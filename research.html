Research

From molecular signal to clinical decision.

Our work sits where biostatistics meets translational oncology. We partner closely with laboratory and clinical teams to design studies, build predictive models from high-dimensional blood-based data, and validate them in independent, prospectively collected cohorts.

01

Lung cancer risk assessment & early detection

Low-dose CT screening saves lives, but eligibility criteria based on age and smoking history miss many people who go on to develop lung cancer. We develop blood-based biomarker panels that, combined with established risk models such as PLCOm2012, identify individuals at elevated risk more precisely — and we quantify what that would mean for screening outcomes and mortality.

Recent work tracks how biomarkers rise in the years before diagnosis, adds circulating metabolites to protein panels, and examines environmental exposures in large screening cohorts.

    02

    Liquid biopsy across cancer types

    Many cancers with the poorest outcomes — pancreatic and ovarian among them — have no effective screening test. Working with proteomics and metabolomics laboratories, we analyse plasma signatures such as polyamines, microbiome-derived metabolites and circulating proteins, and model their lead-time trajectories: how far ahead of clinical diagnosis a marker begins to change.

    The same toolkit extends to risk stratification of pancreatic cysts, prognosis in neuroendocrine tumours of MEN1, and breast cancer risk assessment.

      03

      Statistical & machine-learning methods

      A screening test is only useful if it is highly specific; a classifier tuned for overall accuracy or AUC rarely delivers the best sensitivity at, say, 95% or 99% specificity. We develop methods that optimise directly for the operating point that matters clinically — including SMAGS and its feature-selecting extension SMAGS-LASSO — alongside rank-based learners robust to missing data and small samples, graph neural networks for proteomics, and careful evaluation metrics.

      We also study the design and analysis of screening trials themselves, from sojourn-time estimation to bias in surrogate-endpoint meta-analysis.

        04

        Predicting treatment response & outcome

        Blood drawn before treatment carries information about how a patient is likely to fare. We build predictive panels for durable response and for severe cytokine release syndrome and neurotoxicity after CAR T-cell therapy, apply machine learning to plasma metabolomics to predict response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and validate prognostic lipid panels for men on active surveillance for prostate cancer.
